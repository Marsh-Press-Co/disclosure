Method & honesty

What this is. Every incident on this globe comes from the U.S. government's own released files: the Department of War's PURSUE releases 1–4, the National Archives UAP Records Collection (RG 615), AARO and ODNI reports, and congressional testimony — 384 documents, 12,258 pages, read in full. Text was structured into incident records by a language model with page references; the same-event records of different agencies were matched to measure corroboration.

The skeptic's charter

This site was built by skeptics with no agenda to persuade you in either direction. It relies on government material over private claims — and remains skeptical of the government material too. The files themselves document why: the CIA-convened Robertson Panel (1953) recommended reducing public interest in the subject, and the government's own historical report records decades of managing public perception. Nothing here is presumed true because it is official. Everything is presented as what the record says, with each document's own stance attached and the page number to check it.

Honest counting

★ means 2+ independent government institutions hold paper on the same event — strictly counted: the modern publisher of the releases never counts, renamed agencies (AAF→USAF) are one institution, and press clippings inside files are not agencies. These counts are floors, not ceilings.

Caveats that matter

Most scanned pages were transcribed by a vision model — a re-rendering, not verbatim OCR. Incident fields were extracted by a language model with confidence flags. Both can err; that is why every card links toward its source document. For any quotation or serious use, verify against the source page.

Provenance & credit

All source documents are U.S. government records (public domain, 17 U.S.C. §105). Release 1 transcriptions build on the CC0 ufo-pursue-open-atlas project. This site costs $0 to run, shows no ads, and sets no trackers.
