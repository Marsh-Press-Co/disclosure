APPROVED A CHANGE IN THE FORMULA IT USES TO CALCULATE
INTEREST DUE ON RESCHEDULED DEBT. WE HAVE JUST LEARNED
ABOUT THIS MOVE, APPROVED BY THE BANK'S BOARD OF DIRECTORS
AT THE END OF JUNE, WHICH SHOULD SAVE AFRICAN COUNTRIES
MILLIONS OF DOLLARS IN INTEREST CHARGES OVER THE YEARS.
FORMERLY, THE BANK CHARGED ONE-HALF PERCENT OVER ITS
MEDIUM TERM COST OF FUNDS. FROM NOW ON, HOWEVER, IT WILL
USE THE SAME ONE-HALF PERCENT SPREAD, BUT USE SIX-MONTH
TREASURY BILLS AS ITS BASE RATE. SINCE SHORT TERM
INTEREST RATES ARE GENERALLY BELOW MEDIUM AND LONGER TERM
RATES (ALTHOUGH NOT RECENTLY), THE CHANGE IN THE BASE RATE
AMOUNTS TO AN EFFECTIVE CUT IN EXIM'S INTEREST CHARGES.
THE BANK ESTIMATES THAT THE SAVINGS WILL BE ON THE ORDER
OF 1.5 PERCENTAGE POINTS, OR ALMOST HALF OF THE 3.5
PERCENTAGE POINT REDUCTION CALLED FOR IN OPTION C OF THE
TORONTO "MENU OF OPTIONS" AT THE PARIS CLUB.

SCREWWORM FLY UPDATE: INFORMATION STILL SCANTY. AT AN INTERAGENCY MEETING JULY 27, WE HEARD THE LATEST ON THE SCREWWORM FLY INFESTATION IN LIBYA. A TEAM OF SCREWWORM FLY EXPERTS (LED BY A MEXICAN ENTOMOLOGIST WHO HAS WORKED WITH THE MEXICO-U.S. CONTROL OPERATION) ARRIVED IN LIBYA LAST WEEK UNDER THE AUSPICES OF THE FAO AND INITIATED A SCREWWORM CONTROL TRAINING PROGRAM AND ASSESSMENT OF THE EXTENT OF THE PROBLEM IN LIBYA. THE COUNTRIES BORDERING LIBYA HAVE MOUNTED THEIR OWN INFORMATION CAMPAIGNS AND BORDER SURVEILLANCE ACTIVITIES IN AN EFFORT TO ENSURE THAT THE FLY DOES NOT SPREAD FROM LIBYA. STILL AT ISSUE IS WHETHER OR NOT THE FAO IS UP TO LEADING AN INTERNATIONAL CONTROL PROGRAM. CONSIDERING THE PROHIBITIONS MANY
